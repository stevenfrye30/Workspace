# GLYPHS — CONSTITUTION

The constitution of this room. When in doubt, this file decides.

---

## 1. Working Title

**Glyphs** *(provisional)*.

A name to write into a folder, not a brand to optimize. The permanent name is decided later, by living with the room.

---

## 2. One-Sentence Identity

> **Glyphs is a paleographer's plate book for one reader, attending to how a few specific writing forms descended through history — not a Unicode browser, not a script encyclopedia, not a typography tool, not a calligraphy workshop, not a manuscript reading room.**

---

## 3. Core Metaphor

The room is **a paleographer's plate book**. A small, finite arrangement of dated specimens organized so that descent is visible by leafing.

Properties the room inherits:

- **One scholar uses it.** The reader is singular and known.
- **Each plate shows one specimen.** A single dated form, given room to be looked at.
- **Plates are organized by descent.** The arrangement carries the history.
- **A plate is reverent toward its specimen.** Source attribution, honest dating, scribal context, no clutter.
- **The book is finite.** The plates that exist are the plates that exist. Absence is acceptable.
- **The book is personal but not casual.** The scholar chose what is in it.

What the room is not: a database, a typographic catalogue, a font specimen book, a Unicode index, a paleographic textbook.

---

## 4. Core Principle

> **The dated form is the atom. Descent is the only first-class relation. Lineages emerge from descent; they are not stored.**

A *dated form* is a glyph-shape attested in a particular tradition at a particular approximate time.

*Descent* is the relation between an earlier form and a later form in a documented line of inheritance. Each form has at most one parent and may have several children.

A *lineage* is a path through descent. It is read by walking parent/children chains, not stored as an object.

---

## 5. Temporal Stance

The room is **paleographic and genealogical**.

Paleographic: each form is treated as a specimen — observed carefully, dated honestly with stated uncertainty, sourced to a specific attestation.

Genealogical: forms relate by descent, not by similarity, civilization, language, or function.

The tense the room inhabits is the **active past, traced** — not history-as-narrative, not present-with-historical-footnotes, but the slow archaeological present of looking at one form, then its parent, and noticing what changed.

---

## 6. Minimum Ontology

One entity, one relation. If this fits on an index card, it is the right size.

**Entity:** the *dated form*.

**Relation:** *descent* — parent (zero or one) and children (zero or more).

**Attributes on a form:** the visual specimen itself; a tradition label; an approximate date or date range; a source attribution naming a specific attestation; a brief paleographic note; the reader's own observation, if any.

**Deliberately not entities:**

- The grapheme. The "letter A" abstracted across all its forms is emergent, not stored.
- The script ("Latin"). A tradition tag, not an entity.
- The lineage. A chain is read, not stored.
- The codepoint. Modern Unicode is irrelevant.
- Fonts, typefaces, weights. Modern typography, out of scope.
- The manuscript or inscription as object. A source attribution is a label.
- The phoneme value. A form's sound lives in another room entirely.
- The civilization. Civilizational scope is Atlas.

---

## 7. Initial Scope

The room begins with **one lineage fragment only**: the descent of the letter A from Proto-Sinaitic ʾalp to Phoenician aleph. Two dated forms. Each plated carefully.

Why this fragment:
- Canonical and well-sourced; specimens are publicly studied.
- The most-traveled path in alphabetic descent.
- The room earns its right to exist by treating the foundational descent with care, not by claiming early breadth.

The constitution refuses to begin with multiple lineages, multiple branches, or even the rest of the trunk. **One descent step, fully treated, is the proof of concept.**

---

## 8. What Belongs in v1

- Two dated forms: ʾalp (Proto-Sinaitic) and aleph (Phoenician).
- One plate per form.
- A small lineage view making descent visible at a glance.
- Personal observations attached to plates.
- One stylesheet, shared across all pages.

Nothing else.

---

## 9. What Is Deferred

Not deleted, not condemned, not v1.

- All other letter-lineages.
- All other forms within the A trunk (Greek alpha, Roman A, Carolingian a, modern A).
- All branches off the trunk (Aramaic alef → Arabic, Hebrew, Greek alpha → Cyrillic).
- Brahmic descent.
- Logographic systems entirely. Probably never v1: the atom does not host them cleanly.
- Modern stylistic variations.
- Comparative views across multiple lineages.
- Any geographic or civilizational rendering.
- Any cross-reference to Phonos, SOUND, Atlas, or any other room.

---

## 10. What Must Never Merge

**10a. Must not become a Unicode browser.** No codepoints. No character-by-character browsing. The atom is the historical form, not the encoded character. This is the strongest single refusal in the constitution.

**10b. Must not become a script encyclopedia.** No completionism. Coverage is personal, not exhaustive.

**10c. Must not become a typography or font tool.** No font catalogs, no weights, no OpenType, no modern type design.

**10d. Must not become a calligraphy workshop.** No tracing, no stroke-order practice. Looking, not making.

**10e. Must not merge with Phonos.** A form's phonetic value is invisible here. Greek theta in Glyphs is a shape; theta in Phonos is a sound. Same character; different rooms; different atoms; different attentions. No cross-references in either direction.

**10f. Must not merge with the manuscript room.** Source attributions are labels, not witness-comparison studies.

**10g. Must not merge with Atlas.** No civilizational scope, no geographic distribution, no transmission narratives.

---

## 11. Sourcing Standards

The visual specimen is the content. Sourcing matters more here than in Phonos.

- Each dated form must reference a specific attestation by name — not a generic textbook plate.
- The visual specimen should ideally be drawn or rendered from that attestation, or a public-domain image of it. **Interpretive renderings are acceptable as v1 placeholders if labeled clearly as such.**
- Dates are approximate by default. "c. 1000 BCE" is acceptable; "1000 BCE" without the "c." is overconfident.
- Date ranges are acceptable when consensus is divided.
- Where dating is contested, the plate's source line acknowledges the debate without adjudicating it.
- Tradition labels are specific where it matters.
- The reader's own observation is marked as such — separate from sourced fact.

The room is honest about uncertainty. Forced certainty would be more dishonest than acknowledged ambiguity.

---

## 12. Visual Posture

- Each plate is sparse: one specimen prominent, with date and tradition as small unobtrusive labels, source as a footnote-feeling element.
- The form is the largest thing on the plate. Generous space around it.
- Labels are small. Dates and traditions in small caps or italics.
- Parent and children references are quiet text at the bottom.
- The lineage view is minimal: a simple genealogical arrangement, not a feature-rich graph.
- Pacing is deliberately slow. The room rewards stay-and-compare.
- The visual register is kindred to Phonos, marginally cooler in tone. Same dark navy, no gold accent — a quieter parchment-coloured highlight in its place.

---

## 13. Growth Rules

1. **Add depth before breadth.** A second observation on an existing plate beats a new plate.
2. **Extend the trunk before branching.**
3. **One lineage at a time gets active growth.**
4. **A branch is only added when its trunk is well-treated.**
5. **Sourcing precedes plating.** A plate without a real source is a placeholder, not a plate.
6. **Logographic systems are not added.**
7. **No connection to other rooms.** No cross-links anywhere.
8. **Pace.** At most one new plate per week. The room is maintained at the pace of careful attention; it cannot be batched.
9. **The room may remain incomplete forever.** Most letter-lineages will never be added. That is acceptable.

---

## 14. Open Questions

- **Granularity of "a form."** When does one form become its successor? Decided plate by plate as the room grows.
- **Whether to admit Brahmic descent ever.** Possible, post-v1.
- **Whether to admit logographic systems ever.** Probably not in this room; possibly in a sibling.
- **The room's permanent name.**

---

## 15. The Single Visible Warning

> **Do not add breadth before the room earns it.**

This warning is repeated at the foot of the lineage view. It is the one rule that, if obeyed, allows the room to remain itself. It is the one rule that, if broken, returns the room to the failure mode of every previous attempt.
