# The Discourse on Blessings (Snp 2.4)

## Primary Text

Bhikkhu Sujato, trans., *Anthology of Discourses* (Sutta Nipāta) 2.4, *Maṅgala Sutta*, "Blessings." SuttaCentral, 2021. Dedicated to the public domain (CC0). The Pali root text (Mahāsaṅgīti edition) is held alongside this translation in the Archive library under `sutta-nipata-sujato`.

So I have heard. At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī in Jeta's Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika's monastery. Then, late at night, a glorious deity, lighting up the entire Jeta's Grove, went up to the Buddha, bowed, and stood to one side. Standing to one side, that deity addressed the Buddha in verse:

“Many gods and humans
have thought about blessings
desiring well-being:
declare the highest blessing.”

“Not to fraternize with fools,
but to fraternize with the wise,
and honoring those worthy of honor:
this is the highest blessing.

Living in a suitable region,
having made merit in the past,
being rightly resolved in oneself,
this is the highest blessing.

Education and a craft,
discipline and training,
and well-spoken speech:
this is the highest blessing.

Caring for mother and father,
kindness to children and partners,
and unstressful work:
this is the highest blessing.

Giving and righteous conduct,
kindness to relatives,
blameless deeds:
this is the highest blessing.

Desisting and abstaining from evil,
avoiding drinking liquor,
diligence in good qualities:
this is the highest blessing.

Respect and placidity,
contentment and gratitude,
and timely listening to the teaching:
this is the highest blessing.

Patience, being easy to admonish,
the sight of ascetics,
and timely discussion of the teaching:
this is the highest blessing.

Fervor and chastity,
seeing the noble truths,
and realization of extinguishment:
this is the highest blessing.

Though touched by worldly things,
their mind does not tremble;
sorrowless, stainless, secure,
this is the highest blessing.

Having completed these things,
undefeated everywhere;
everywhere they go in safety:
this is their highest blessing.”

## Atlas notes

The *Maṅgala Sutta* answers an old and very ordinary question — what is the truly auspicious thing, the real blessing? — that in the Buddha's day drew long debates over lucky sights, sounds, and omens. The Buddha sets all of that aside and gives instead a graded list of conduct, beginning where anyone can begin: keep good company, avoid fools, honor what deserves honor. From there it climbs — through skill and discipline, care for parents and family, generosity and blameless work, restraint and gratitude, patience and the company of the wise — until it reaches the summit: seeing the four noble truths, realizing extinguishment, and a mind that "does not tremble" though touched by the world's gains and losses. The "blessing" turns out to be nothing external at all, but a life built well from the ground up.

## Context

The discourse belongs to the Sutta Nipāta in the Khuddaka Nikāya, and is one of the canon's principal *parittā* — protective recitations chanted for safety and good fortune at weddings, house-blessings, and new ventures across the Theravada world. Its frame is conventional for such texts: a radiant deity comes by night to Jeta's Grove (the monastery donated by the merchant Anāthapiṇḍika near Sāvatthī) and puts the question in verse. Its enduring popularity comes from its accessibility — it is an ethics anyone can act on, arranged as a path rather than a creed.

The translation reproduced above is Bhikkhu Sujato's, released to the public domain; the Pali root text is preserved beside it in the Archive library.

## Reading note

Read the list as a staircase, not a heap. The verses are roughly ordered from the most basic and social (whom you associate with, how you treat your family) to the most inward and advanced (an unshakable mind, the realization of *nibbāna*). Each rung is called "the highest blessing" because, at the level one is on, it genuinely is. The discourse's quiet argument is that fortune is made, not found — and that the ascent begins with something as simple as the company one keeps.
