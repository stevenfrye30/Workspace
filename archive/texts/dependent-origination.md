# Dependent Origination (SN 12.1–12.2)

## Primary Text

Bhikkhu Sujato, trans., *Linked Discourses* (Saṃyutta Nikāya) 12.2 (*Vibhaṅga*, "Analysis") and 12.1 (*Paṭiccasamuppāda*). SuttaCentral, 2018. Dedicated to the public domain (CC0). The Pali root text is held alongside this translation in the Archive library under `linked-discourses-sujato`.

*The arising (SN 12.2):*

“And what is dependent origination? Ignorance is a requirement for choices. Choices are a requirement for consciousness. Consciousness is a requirement for name and form. Name and form are requirements for the six sense fields. The six sense fields are requirements for contact. Contact is a requirement for feeling. Feeling is a requirement for craving. Craving is a requirement for grasping. Grasping is a requirement for continued existence. Continued existence is a requirement for rebirth. Rebirth is a requirement for old age and death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress to come to be. That is how this entire mass of suffering originates.”

*The ceasing (SN 12.1):*

“When ignorance fades away and ceases with no residue left behind, choices cease. When choices cease, consciousness ceases. When consciousness ceases, name and form cease. When name and form cease, the six sense fields cease. When the six sense fields cease, contact ceases. When contact ceases, feeling ceases. When feeling ceases, craving ceases. When craving ceases, grasping ceases. When grasping ceases, continued existence ceases. When continued existence ceases, rebirth ceases. When rebirth ceases, old age and death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress cease. That is how this entire mass of suffering ceases.”

## Atlas notes

Dependent origination (*paṭicca-samuppāda*) is the theoretical heart of the Buddha's teaching — his account of *why* there is suffering and *how* it can end, stated not as a story of creation or a first cause but as a chain of conditions. Its general principle is stark: *when this exists, that comes to be; from the arising of this, that arises.* Nothing in the chain is a self or a maker; each link simply leans on the one before. Run forward, the twelve links trace how suffering is manufactured — ignorance shapes our intentional activity, which conditions consciousness, which together with "name and form" (mind and body) gives rise to the six senses, to contact, to feeling, to craving, to grasping, to continued existence, to rebirth, and so to ageing and death and the whole mass of grief. Run backward — remove ignorance, and the chain unwinds, link by link, to its end. This is the same insight the four noble truths compress: the second truth (craving as the origin of suffering) and the third (its cessation) are this chain read forwards and backwards. The tradition holds it in the highest regard and the deepest caution: "whoever sees dependent origination sees the Dhamma," Sāriputta said — and the Buddha warned that few teachings are so easy to misunderstand.

## Context

These two discourses open the *Nidānasaṃyutta*, the "connected discourses on causation," in the Saṃyutta Nikāya — the Buddha's most sustained treatment of the subject, expanded in the *Mahānidāna Sutta* (DN 15). No doctrine in Buddhism has drawn more commentary: the classical Theravada reads the twelve links across three lifetimes (past causes, present results and causes, future results), while other readings stress how the whole sequence can be seen in a single moment of grasping. Across all of them, it remains the framework within which karma, rebirth, and liberation are understood.

The translation reproduced above is Bhikkhu Sujato's; the Pali root text is preserved beside it in the Archive library.

## Reading note

Read it as conditionality, not fate, and not as a self travelling down a chain. Each link makes the next *possible*, not inevitable, and no one stands behind them pulling the thread. The most practical place to stand is the hinge between **feeling** and **craving**: every other link is largely set in motion already, but here, in the gap where a pleasant or painful feeling is about to harden into wanting or rejecting, the chain can actually be interrupted in real time. That gap is exactly where mindfulness (the *Satipaṭṭhāna*) does its work — which is why insight into dependent origination and the practice of mindfulness are two ends of the same tool.
